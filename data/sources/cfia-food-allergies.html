Before you shop: food allergies and allergen labelling

To help people with allergies and related disorders make informed choices, prepackaged food labels must identify priority allergens and gluten sources.

Priority allergens

In Canada, the most common allergens in food are known as priority allergens, and must be clearly identified on prepackaged food labels.

These priority allergens are:

  • eggs
  • milk
  • mustard
  • peanuts
  • crustaceans and molluscs
  • fish
  • sesame seeds
  • soy
  • sulphites
  • tree nuts (almonds, Brazil nuts, cashews, hazelnuts, macadamia nuts, pecans, pine nuts, pistachios and walnuts)
  • wheat and triticale

Gluten sources

Gluten is found in the grains of certain cereals and hybrid crops grown from those cereals. Like priority allergens, gluten sources must be identified on prepackaged food labels.

The grains that are gluten sources are:

  • barley
  • oats
  • rye
  • triticale
  • wheat

Rules for food labels

Prepackaged food products that contain priority food allergens and gluten sources must include them in the list of ingredients and/or in a statement that begins with "Contains" on their label. For example, a product with peanuts would include the word "peanuts" in the list of ingredients, or in a statement that reads "Contains peanuts," or both.

These allergens and gluten sources must be declared by their prescribed source names, such as, "egg" and "wheat", so consumers can easily recognize them. For example, if there is flour in the food product, the company would need to include "wheat" in brackets, or include a statement that reads, "Contains wheat."

Precautionary statements

Precautionary statements alert consumers about the possible presence of an allergen(s) in food due to cross-contamination. Companies may use a precautionary statement using the words "May contain" if there is a possibility that a food allergen or gluten source may be present despite all reasonable measures to prevent cross-contamination. These statements are not a substitute for good manufacturing practices.

Cross-contamination statements must not be used when an allergen, allergen-containing ingredient or gluten source is intentionally added to a food.

Examples of how allergens are labelled

Allergens can appear on food labels in two ways: within the ingredients list, or in a separate "Contains" statement after the ingredients.

Within the ingredients list

Ingredients: Apples, Pie crust [flour (wheat), shortening, liquid albumen (egg), salt], Sugar, Flour (wheat), Lemon juice, Whole milk, Cinnamon.
May contain pecans.

Or

Using a contains statement

Ingredients: Apples, Pie crust (flour, shortening, liquid albumen, salt), Sugar, Flour, Lemon juice, Whole milk, Cinnamon. Contains: Wheat, Egg, Milk.
May contain pecans.

 

If you have a food allergy or related disorder

If you or someone in your family has a food allergy or related disorder, there are two important steps you can take to protect them: check product labels for allergens or gluten sources every time you shop, and sign up for food recall notifications so that you are alerted when a product with undeclared allergens or gluten sources is recalled.

  1. Check the label every time

    Check for allergens or gluten sources on the product label each time you buy a product. Companies can sometimes change ingredients, so it's essential to check labels each time products are purchased. If you or someone you know has an allergy that is not one of the priority allergens, you will need to read the list of ingredients carefully and contact the company if you are unsure if it contains a certain ingredient.

  2. Sign up for food recalls

    When the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) becomes aware of a potential serious hazard associated with a food, such as undeclared allergens or gluten sources, the agency investigates and takes action to protect consumers, which may include requesting a recall of the food product. You can find out about food recalls by:

    • checking the recalls and safety alerts
    • signing up for email notifications
    • following us on Facebook or X
